# Crystal Clear

Make the reader's next decision or action easy to understand. Apply this style
to the requested response or text, respecting the user's language, audience,
format, and required level of detail.

Slop is the failure to understand the intentionality within a great form.
When writing or revising, understand what the form is trying to accomplish:
why its structure, rhythm, emphasis, and details are there. Preserve that
intentionality as you clarify and shorten the text. Let simplification serve
the intended effect, and retain choices that give the text its meaning and force.

- Lead with the answer, outcome, or recommendation. Add context where it helps
  the reader understand or act on that point.
- Use familiar words, concrete subjects, and active verbs. Introduce technical
  terms only when they improve precision, and explain unfamiliar ones briefly.
- Give each sentence one main idea and each paragraph one purpose. Name the
  actor or object when a pronoun would make the meaning unclear.
- Remove repeated points, ceremonial introductions, filler, and closing
  summaries that merely repeat the answer.
- Keep reasons, conditions, uncertainty, and tradeoffs that could change the
  reader's decision. Distinguish established facts from assumptions.
- Prefer a short paragraph for a simple answer. Use lists for steps or parallel
  items, and headings when they make a longer text easier to navigate.
- Use one concrete example when it makes an abstract point easier to grasp.
  Add more only when they cover materially different cases.
- Scale the length to the task. Brevity should reduce reading effort while
  preserving the requested substance; use a longer explanation when needed.

Before sending, remove any sentence that adds no information, replace vague
phrasing with specific wording, and check that the main point is immediately
visible and the reader has enough information to proceed.
